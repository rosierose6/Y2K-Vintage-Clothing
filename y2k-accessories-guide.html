Y2K Accessories: Butterfly Clips, Chokers & Tiny Bags

A Y2K Vintage Clothing styling guide · 6 min read

Accessories are where Y2K actually happens. You can build the whole outfit — the baby tee, the low-rise jeans, the mini skirt — and it will still read as "cute top and jeans" until something small and slightly ridiculous goes in your hair. That's the part people remember, and it's the part that costs the least to get right secondhand.

Here's what's worth hunting for, how to spot the real thing in a thrift bin, and how to wear it now without it tipping into costume.

Butterfly clips

The single most recognisable Y2K accessory, and the easiest to get wrong. The original clips were small — think an inch across — with a little spring-loaded claw and iridescent or frosted wings. Modern reproductions tend to be bigger, glossier, and made of a harder plastic that catches the light differently.

How to wear them now: odd numbers, scattered asymmetrically, rather than a neat symmetrical row. Three or five clips tucked into a half-up section looks intentional. Twelve clips in a perfect arc looks like a school disco. If your hair is dark, iridescent clips show up beautifully; if it's light, go for a colour rather than a clear frost.

Ribbon chokers & tattoo necklaces

Two very different moods from the same era. A satin or velvet ribbon tied at the throat is the softer, more romantic option — it's the piece that lets a rhinestone tee lean fairycore instead of nightclub. The stretchy black "tattoo" choker is the harder-edged cousin, and it looks best when the rest of the outfit is simple.

Ribbon is the one accessory genuinely worth making rather than buying. A metre of good velvet ribbon from a haberdashery costs almost nothing and you can cut it to your own neck, which is more than you can say for most vintage chokers.

The tiny shoulder bag

Small, structured, worn high under the arm on a short strap — this is the silhouette that dates an outfit to about 2002 faster than anything else. Look for baguette shapes, top-handle minis, and anything in a shiny or beaded finish. Secondhand is by far the best route here: the vintage ones have better hardware and the proportions are correct in a way the revival versions rarely are.

What to check before you buy: the zip (the most common failure point), the strap join where it meets the body, and the lining at the base corners. Everything else on a vintage bag is usually fixable; a broken zip on a tiny bag rarely is.

Sunglasses

Narrow, slightly rectangular, tinted rather than blacked out. Rose, amber, and pale blue lenses all read early-2000s. Wire frames feel a bit more grown-up; chunky coloured plastic goes full 2001. If you find a pair with the lenses intact and the hinges tight, take them — the frames are the hard part, and any optician can swap lenses.

The ones people forget

Beaded or shell chokers and bracelets. Cheap, everywhere in thrift stores, and instantly softening. They pair especially well with the fairycore end of the wardrobe.

Thin scarves worn as belts or hair ties. A silk square through the belt loops is one of the easiest ways to add colour without adding another garment.

Layered thin necklaces. Two or three fine chains at different lengths — this is the detail that makes a simple tee look styled rather than thrown on.

The one-loud-thing rule. Pick a single accessory to be the statement and let everything else stay quiet. Butterfly clips or the beaded choker or the shiny bag — not all three at once. This is the difference between an outfit that looks put together and one that looks like a themed party, and it's the same principle that makes the rest of the Y2K wardrobe work.

A note on buying secondhand

Accessories are the friendliest place to start thrifting, because fit isn't a factor. There's no sizing to guess at, no measurements to cross-reference — if you like it and the clasp works, it's yours. It's also the cheapest way to test whether an aesthetic actually suits you before committing to the clothes.

The trade-off is that small things are the first to get damaged and the first to disappear. Check clasps, springs, and zips before anything else, and buy the piece when you see it. With one-of-one accessories especially, there's no coming back for it next week.
